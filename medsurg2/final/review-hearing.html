Hearing and Balance Disorders

Key terms

TermDefinition
DizzinessAny altered sense of orientation in space
VertigoIllusion of motion or spinning sensation
NystagmusInvoluntary rhythmic movement of the eyes
TinnitusRinging in the ear — often from loud sounds; common in veterans

External ear conditions

  • Cerumen impaction — ear wax buildup causing hearing loss/discomfort; removed by irrigation
  • External otitis ("swimmer's ear") — pain on the external ear, edema/discharge; antibiotics; untreated → permanent hearing loss. Keep ear dry, no swimming, no objects in canal.
  • Chronic otitis media — results from recurrent acute otitis media

Ototoxicity

Damage to cochlea/vestibular system from medications. Tinnitus is often the first sign — report immediately, damage can be permanent. Hearing loss affects high frequencies first.

CategoryExamples
AminoglycosidesGentamicin, tobramycin, streptomycin — key risk
Loop diureticsFurosemide
ChemotherapyCisplatin, carboplatin
SalicylatesHigh-dose aspirin
OtherQuinine
Never combine ototoxic drugse.g. furosemide + an aminoglycoside together significantly raises permanent hearing damage risk.

Vertigo disorders

  • Ménière's disease — triad: episodic vertigo + tinnitus + unilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Low-sodium diet, avoid caffeine/alcohol/nicotine, Meclizine. During attack: lie down, stay still, quiet environment, fall precautions.
  • BPPV — brief incapacitating vertigo triggered by head position change. Dix-Hallpike diagnoses it (reproduces vertigo); Epley maneuver treats it (repositions displaced crystals). Fall precautions = priority nursing intervention.
  • Labyrinthitis — sudden vertigo + N/V ± tinnitus/partial hearing loss. Meclizine, fall precautions, bed rest acute phase, antiemetics, quiet environment.

Communication & safety

  • Face the patient, get attention first, reduce background noise, low-tone normal voice (don't shout), speak slowly/distinctly, direct speech to the less impaired ear
  • Written information or sign language interpreter if needed
  • Safety: call light within reach, visual/vibrating alert systems (alarm clocks, doorbells), fall precautions if vestibular involvement
  • Hearing aids: remove at night, keep dry, dry-cloth cleaning, avoid hairspray near device, check battery regularly
