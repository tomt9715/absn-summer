Hepatic Disorders

The professor's framing (still applies)

Focus: signs/symptoms, nursing interventions, what we're monitoring for, liver function tests, keeping ammonia levels down. There will be a lactulose question.

Ammonia normal: 10–80 mcg/dL. AST/ALT elevation = liver cell damage indicator.

Physical assessment findings

  • Jaundice — yellow/greenish skin & sclerae from elevated bilirubin
  • Spider angiomas, palmar erythema, petechiae/ecchymoses
  • Male patients: gynecomastia, testicular atrophy (hormonal changes)
  • Clay-colored stool, tea-colored urine — bile flow disruption
  • Asterixis — flapping tremor, wrist dorsiflexion, stage II hepatic encephalopathy

Portal hypertension → ascites & varices

Obstructed liver blood flow → increased portal venous pressure → ascites (sodium/water retention + ↓albumin synthesis + increased lymphatic flow) + esophageal varices (blood reroutes through fragile vessels not built for the volume).

Ascites managementLow-sodium diet, diuretics, bed rest, paracentesis, salt-poor albumin, TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt) for refractory cases.
Esophageal varices bleeding = emergencyUrgent endoscopy, NPO, sclerotherapy or banding therapy.

Hepatic encephalopathy

Earliest signs: mental status changes, motor disturbances, day/night sleep reversal → confusion → difficult to arouse → coma/seizures.

NAFLD causes: excessive fructose, gut dysbiosis, standard American diet.

Cirrhosis staging & management

StageFindings
EarlyEnlarged liver, jaundice, GI disturbances, weight loss
LateSmall/nodular liver, splenomegaly, ascites, bleeding tendencies, pruritus, clay stool, tea urine
End stageSlurred speech, vacant stare, asterixis, apraxia → stupor → coma (high mortality)
  • Meds: spironolactone/furosemide (diuretics), neomycin/metronidazole (reduce intestinal ammonia-producing bacteria), lactulose, vitamins B/A/C/K/folic acid/thiamine
  • Diet: high-calorie, low-protein (20–40g/day), low-fat, low-sodium
  • Avoid: hepatotoxic meds — acetaminophen especially
  • Monitor: liver enzymes, bilirubin, CBC, PT/INR, ammonia; daily weight + abdominal girth
Acute hepatic failureSudden severe liver failure within 8 weeks of first jaundice symptoms (formerly "fulminant hepatic failure").
