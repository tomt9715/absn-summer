Kidney Disorders

What the kidneys do

Remove waste + excess fluid, regulate sodium/potassium/acid balance, produce erythropoietin (RBC production), activate vitamin D (bone health), and help regulate BP.

Most accurate fluid indicatorDaily weight, not I&O totals — I&O underestimates insensible losses/gains.

Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

Diagnostic criterion50%+ rise in serum creatinine above baseline = AKI.

Urine output categories: Nonoliguria >800 mL/day · Oliguria <0.5 mL/kg/hr · Anuria <50 mL/day

Hyperkalemia = most life-threatening complicationRestrict bananas, citrus, coffee. Calcium gluconate, insulin+D50, Kayexalate as needed.

Glomerulonephritis & Nephrotic Syndrome

  • Acute glomerulonephritis — post-strep → penicillin if residual strep suspected. High protein → increases intraglomerular pressure, worsens damage.
  • Chronic glomerulonephritis — yellow-gray skin pigmentation, poorly nourished, periorbital/peripheral edema, anemia (pale mucous membranes), confusion in advanced disease
  • Nephrotic syndrome — proteinuria >3.5g/day is the hallmark. Diffuse edema, foamy urine, hyperlipidemia → accelerated atherosclerosis risk. ACE inhibitors reduce proteinuria; diuretics for edema.
  • Nephrotic complications: infection (deficient immune response), thromboembolism (renal vein), AKI from hypovolemia

ESKD complications

  • Anemia — decreased erythropoietin production
  • Bone disease — phosphorus retention + low calcium + abnormal vitamin D + elevated aluminum
  • Hypertension — sodium/water retention + RAAS malfunction
  • Cardiac — pericarditis, pericardial effusion, tamponade (uremic waste retention)

Hemodialysis & vascular access

  • AVF (arteriovenous fistula) — preferred permanent access, surgically joins artery to vein, needs 2–3 months to mature before use
  • AV graft used if AVF isn't appropriate
  • Assess bruit/thrill every shift; absent = possible clot/blockage. Never use fistula arm for BP or blood draws.
  • Hold antihypertensives before dialysis — prevents combined hypotension
  • Dialysis disequilibrium — cerebral fluid shifts, first treatment risk: headache, nausea, restlessness, possible seizures
  • Uremia → metallic taste + nausea
  • Skin care: manage pruritus, moisturize, trim nails
Peritoneal dialysisCloudy effluent + abdominal pain = suspect peritonitis, culture the fluid.

Kidney surgery & transplant

  • Pre-op: encourage fluids to promote waste excretion; nephrotoxic antibiotics used with extreme caution if infection present
  • Post-nephrectomy priority: hemorrhage and shock are the main complications
  • Paralytic ileus common after renal surgery → NG decompression; oral fluids resume once flatus passes
  • Transplant = treatment of choice for ESKD, elective (not emergency), costs ⅓ of dialysis; post-op watch for bleeding, pneumonia, infection, DVT — early ambulation and leg exercises prevent DVT
