Fluid & Electrolytes

The professor's framing (still applies)

Test focus is signs/symptoms, causes/risk factors, and patient education — not granular mechanism or every lab value. Hallmark signs (Trousseau's, Chvostek's, ECG changes) are fair game.

Sodium (135–145 mEq/L)

Hyponatremia (<135)Hypernatremia (>145)
RiskFluid overload (acute, surgical), vomiting/diarrheaDehydration, GI loss, DI, kidney failure, burns, corticosteroids
S/SConfusion, cramps; critically low → seizure precautionsThirst, fever, hallucinations, irritability, seizures, tachycardia
TeachingSodium-rich foods: broth, canned beans, salted nutsIncrease fluid intake; watch OTC products with sodium
Elderly + hypernatremiaDecreased thirst sensation with aging → schedule fluids proactively, don't wait for thirst.

Potassium (3.5–5.0 mEq/L)

Hypokalemia (<3.5)Hyperkalemia (>5.0)
RiskLoop diuretics, laxative abuse, vomiting/diarrhea, NG suctionRenal failure, potassium-sparing diuretics + ACE inhibitors together, acidosis
ECGFlat/inverted T, prominent U wavePeaked T waves first, then widened QRS, absent P
TeachingBananas, melon, citrus, lean meat, milkLow-K foods: butter, cranberry juice, ginger ale, hard candy — check cola labels
Hyperkalemia med sequenceCalcium gluconate stabilizes the heart FIRST (doesn't lower K+) → then Kayexalate / insulin+glucose / diuretics actually remove or shift potassium.

Calcium (8.8–10.4 mg/dL)

HypocalcemiaHypercalcemia
RiskHypoparathyroidism, hypomagnesemia, kidney failure, vit D deficiencyMalignancy, hyperparathyroidism, immobility, thiazides
S/STetany, paresthesia, +Trousseau's/+Chvostek'sPolyuria, thirst, bone/flank pain, hyporeflexia, heart blocks
TxIV calcium gluconate, seizure precautionsIV isotonic saline + fluids 2.8–3.8L/day; calcitonin if can't tolerate sodium load
Trousseau's vs. Chvostek'sTrousseau's = carpal spasm when BP cuff inflated. Chvostek's = facial muscle contraction when facial nerve tapped. Both positive = hypocalcemia.

Magnesium & Phosphate

  • Hypomagnesemia — alcoholism, GI loss, DKA, acute MI; often travels WITH hypokalemia/hypocalcemia; +Chvostek's/Trousseau's; ECG: Torsades, V-fib risk
  • Hypermagnesemia — kidney injury (kidneys normally excrete it well); loss of DTRs = stop the infusion and notify provider
  • Hypophosphatemia — alcoholism, refeeding after starvation, DKA, major burns; confusion, muscle weakness, seizures
  • Hyperphosphatemia — kidney injury; symptoms are really from the resulting hypocalcemia (reciprocal relationship); avoid Coca-Cola/processed foods

Chloride (97–107 mmol/L)

  • Hypochloremia — gastric losses (vomiting), follows low sodium, DKA → agitation, tremors, seizures
  • Hyperchloremia — bicarb loss (kidney/GI), seen with hypernatremia and metabolic acidosis → tachypnea, weakness, coma

Fluid volume & third spacing

  • FVD/hypovolemia — acute weight loss, oliguria, poor skin turgor (unreliable in elderly) → isotonic fluids (NS, LR)
  • FVE/hypervolemia — heart failure, AKI, cirrhosis → edema, JVD, crackles; question any additional isotonic bolus order
  • Third spacing — fluid trapped where it can't equilibrate: ascites, pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, angioedema. Patient looks overloaded but is functionally hypovolemic.
  • D5W — isotonic in the bag but metabolizes to free water → avoid in head injury (cerebral edema risk) and children
  • Hypertonic saline (3%) — severe symptomatic hyponatremia, cerebral edema

ABG interpretation — tic-tac-toe method

ValueNormal
pH7.35–7.45
PaCO235–45 (acid if high, base if low — inverted!)
HCO3-22–26
  1. Plot pH, PaCO2, HCO3- into Acid/Normal/Base columns
  2. pH + PaCO2 same column → respiratory. pH + HCO3- same column → metabolic
  3. Uncompensated = 3rd value still normal. Partial = pH still abnormal, both others abnormal. Full = pH normal, both others still abnormal (check which side pH leans toward for the primary problem)
Classic causesResp acidosis = opioid OD/hypoventilation. Resp alkalosis = anxiety or salicylate toxicity. Metabolic acidosis = DKA (Kussmaul breathing) or diarrhea. Metabolic alkalosis = vomiting or antacid overdose.
