Shock, Sepsis & MODS

The throughline

Shock = inadequate tissue perfusion to deliver oxygen/nutrients. Compensatory = body is winning, BP still normal but everything else already abnormal. Progressive = compensation failing, BP finally drops. Irreversible = compensation has failed completely, organs shutting down, nothing works without mechanical/pharmacologic support.

Earliest sign isn't BPHR rises above 100 in compensatory stage while BP is still normal — this is the trap. Watch HR, not just BP.
The acid-base flipCompensatory = respiratory alkalosis (fast breathing blows off CO2). Progressive = metabolic acidosis (cells go anaerobic, produce lactate — ties to the lactate >2 sepsis trigger). Genuine reversal, not just "worse of the same."

Skin progression: cold/clammy (compensatory) → mottled/petechiae (progressive) → jaundice (irreversible, liver failure).

MAP

MAP must exceed 65 mmHg to sustain lifeVasoactive meds are added when fluids alone can't maintain this target.

Classification of shock

TypeMechanism
Distributive — SepticDysregulated response to severe infection

HypovolemicMost common — decreased intravascular volume

First-line treatment by type — know these, they're different

TypeFirst-line
HypovolemicIV fluids (crystalloids, then blood if hemorrhagic)
CardiogenicOxygen — NOT aggressive fluids, can worsen it
NeurogenicVasopressors + spinal immobilization; atropine for bradycardia
AnaphylacticEpinephrine IM — never delay for antihistamines
SepticAntibiotics (after cultures) + 30 mL/kg fluid bolus
Anaphylaxis orderEpi IM (thigh) first → call for help → O2/prepare for intubation → IV fluids → diphenhydramine (H1) + famotidine (H2) → corticosteroids (prevents biphasic reaction) → bronchodilators for bronchospasm.

Vasoactive medications — the 3 ICU rules

  • VS monitored at least every 15 min until stable
  • Central line only — never peripheral (caustic/concentrated, extravasation causes tissue necrosis)
  • Nurse titrates independently to the prescribed target (usually MAP ≥65)
MedUse
Norepinephrine (Levophed)First-line vasopressor for septic shock
DopamineDose-dependent effects, titrate carefully
DobutamineIncreases myocardial contractility — watch for chest pain (increases O2 demand)

Nutrition & psychosocial support

  • Start enteral nutrition within 24–48 hrs if hemodynamically stable — enteral > parenteral (preserves gut, lowers infection risk)
  • High protein: 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day; glucose target 140–180 mg/dL
  • Family: frequent honest communication, no jargon, allow bedside presence, involve chaplaincy/palliative care early, never false reassurance

MODS

Definition: altered function in 2+ organ systems. Most common with sepsis, toward the end of the septic shock continuum.

Shock typeMODS driver
SepticLactate ≥2 is the trigger for protocol
CardiogenicOxygen delivery failure

Older adults highest risk — less physiologic reserve, immune compromise.
