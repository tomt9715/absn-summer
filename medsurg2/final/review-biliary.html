Biliary Disorders

The gallbladder & enterohepatic circulation

Gallbladder = storage depot for bile. Bile salts + cholesterol emulsify fats in the distal ileum, get reabsorbed into portal blood, return to the liver, get re-excreted into bile. This loop is the enterohepatic circulation.

When bile flow is blocked (gallstones)Bilirubin can't enter the intestine → blood bilirubin rises → increased renal excretion of urobilinogen (tea-colored urine) + decreased excretion in stool (clay-colored stool).

Cholelithiasis & cholecystitis

Gallstones = hardened bile deposits, mostly cholesterol stones (~75% of cases) from excess cholesterol in bile.

Risk factors: low-dose OR high-dose estrogen therapy, obesity, rapid weight loss, women (especially multiple pregnancies).

Cholecystitis presentationRUQ pain/tenderness/rigidity radiating to midsternal or right shoulder, N/V, worse after fatty meals. Empyema (purulent gallbladder) = serious complication — fever, chills, worsening pain.

ERCP

  • NPO since midnight; baseline VS + med reconciliation before
  • Glucagon given — decreases duodenal peristalsis, makes cannulation easier
  • Moderate sedation → monitor closely for respiratory depression, hypotension, vomiting during the procedure
  • Post-op: confirm normal neuro status + intact gag/cough reflex before oral intake
LithotripsyNoninvasive — repeated shock waves fragment stones so they pass more easily, no incision needed.

Diet & post-op cholecystectomy

  • Immediately after an episode: low-fat liquids
  • Ongoing: avoid eggs, cream, pork, fried foods, cheese, rich dressings, gas-forming vegetables, alcohol
  • Post-op priorities: relieve pain, improve respiratory status, maintain skin integrity, promote biliary drainage, improve nutrition, monitor complications
  • JP drain no output → check tubing for kinks first, then call surgeon
  • Referred shoulder pain post-lap chole = retained CO2 from insufflation, not a new injury — encourage deep breathing/incentive spirometer

Pancreatitis

AcuteChronic
PathoDuct obstructed → enzymes back up → autodigestion/inflammationProgressive fibrosis replaces cells → pressure rises → obstructs pancreatic AND common bile duct
Classic presentationSevere midepigastric pain, 24–48 hrs after heavy meal/alcohol, diffuse and hard to localize, worse after meals, unrelieved by antacids. Abdominal distention + poorly defined palpable mass. Decreased peristalsis, vomiting doesn't relieve it.

Labs: amylase/lipase elevated within 24 hrs, WBC elevated, hypocalcemia correlates with severity.

Management: NPO, opioid analgesia (morphine/hydromorphone/fentanyl via PCA), IV fluids, gradual oral reintroduction. Between attacks: high-protein, low-fat diet, avoid heavy meals + alcohol. Chronic = smoking, alcohol, malnutrition are the major causes.
