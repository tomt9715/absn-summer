Cerebrovascular Disorders (Stroke)

Time is brain

Stroke = sudden loss of function from disrupted blood supply to the brain. The single biggest factor in outcome is how fast you act.

TIATemporary neurologic deficit — a "warning of impending stroke." Even if symptoms fully resolve, it needs a full diagnostic workup.

Ischemic vs. Hemorrhagic

Ischemic (87%)Hemorrhagic (13%)
CauseLarge artery thrombosis, small penetrating artery thrombosis, cardioembolic (e.g. A-fib), cryptogenic (no known cause)Intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, cerebral aneurysm, AVM
Main signNumbness/weakness face/arm/leg, especially one side"Exploding headache," decreased LOC
Recovery plateau~6 monthsSlower — ~18 months
FASTFace drooping · Arm weakness (drift) · Speech difficulty · Time to call 911 (note exact onset time)

Risk factors

Nonmodifiable: age >55, male gender

Modifiable: hypertension (PRIMARY risk factor), CAD, elevated cholesterol, obesity, diabetes, oral contraceptives, smoking/drug/alcohol use

Ischemic stroke — assessment & manifestations

  • Numbness/weakness face/arm/leg (one side), confusion, trouble speaking/understanding, difficulty walking/dizziness/loss of coordination, sudden severe headache, perceptual disturbances
  • Initial assessment: airway patency first (gag/cough reflex may be compromised), then cardiovascular status (BP, rhythm, carotid bruit), then gross neuro deficits
  • Deficit categories to know: visual field, motor, sensory, verbal, cognitive, emotional
Code Gray workflowNoncontrast CT within 25 minutes or less — distinguishes ischemic vs hemorrhagic, drives treatment. Patient currently in CT is removed; stroke patient becomes priority.

Thrombolytic therapy (tPA) — ischemic only

  • Given within 3 hours of symptom onset — decreases stroke size, improves 3-month outcome
  • Door-to-needle goal: within 60 minutes of arrival
  • Contraindicated in hemorrhagic stroke — would worsen the bleed
  • Bleeding is the most common side effect

Hemorrhagic stroke

Bleeding into brain tissue, ventricles, or subarachnoid space. Key distinguishing sign vs ischemic = the exploding headache. Blood in subarachnoid space → rising ICP (added volume in a closed skull).

Secondary prevention

  • A-fib/cardioembolic stroke → dose-adjusted warfarin
  • Statin recommended even if LDL <100 and no known atherosclerotic disease — stabilizes plaque
  • Control blood pressure = top modifiable target
