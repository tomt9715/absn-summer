Burns

Who's at risk & burn depth

Young children and older adults — thin skin means deeper damage from the same exposure.

DepthAppearancePainHealing
Superficial (1st)Red, dry, no blistersPainful3–5 days, no scar
Superficial partial (2nd)Moist, red, blistersVery painful7–21 days, minimal scar
Deep partial (2nd)Pale, mottled, wetLess pain (nerve damage)>21 days, significant scar; often needs grafting
Full thickness (3rd)Leathery, dry, white/brown/blackPainless (nerves destroyed)No spontaneous healing — grafting required

Rule of Nines & Parkland Formula

Head 9% · each arm 9% · anterior trunk 18% · posterior trunk 18% · each leg 18% · perineum 1%

Parkland Formula4 mL × weight (kg) × %TBSA burned = total fluid, first 24 hrs. Half in first 8 hours FROM TIME OF BURN (not arrival), other half over next 16 hrs. Titrate to UO 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr.
Curreri Formula (nutrition)25 kcal/kg/day + 40 kcal/%TBSA burned. High protein 1.5–2.5 g/kg/day. Supplement vitamin C, zinc, vitamin A, B-complex. Start enteral nutrition within 6 hours of major burn.

Emergent phase (ED)

  • Foley catheter inserted; NG tube for burns >20–25% TBSA (ileus prevention/decompression)
  • Fluid resuscitation for burns >20% TBSA — LR preferred; central venous access preferred for larger burns (large volume needed)
  • Croup-like brassy cough → O2 + continuous pulse ox. Progressive hoarseness + audible wheezing/stridor → prepare for intubation
  • Inhalation injury: supraglottic (heat) vs subglottic (chemical); singed nasal hairs, carbonaceous sputum, facial burns = red flags
  • CO poisoning → pulse ox is unreliable (reads falsely normal) → 100% O2 non-rebreather + carboxyhemoglobin level

Zones of injury

ZoneStatus
CoagulationDirect contact — irreversible necrosis
StasisSurrounding — ischemic but salvageable IF resuscitated adequately
HyperemiaOutermost — inflammation only, fully recoverable
Critical conceptInadequate fluid resuscitation converts zone of stasis → coagulation, deepening the burn.

Acute phase — infection, debridement, escharotomy

  • Wounds cultured by biopsy (not surface swab) on admission, each surgical case, and clinical suspicion of infection
  • No prophylactic systemic antibiotics — promotes resistance; treatment is culture-guided
  • Immunosuppression window: 3–5 days post-burn
  • Silver sulfadiazine avoided on the face — bacitracin preferred (less systemic absorption)
  • Premedicate 30 min before wound care/debridement — extremely painful procedure
  • Escharotomy — circumferential full-thickness burns of extremities OR chest → prevents compartment syndrome / respiratory restriction

Systemic effects

  • Cardiovascular: burn shock first 48 hrs — capillary leak → hypovolemia, ↓CO, ↑SVR
  • Metabolic: hypermetabolic 2–3x basal rate; hypothermia risk from open wounds → warm room 28–32°C, warming blankets, warmed IV fluids
  • Renal: myoglobinuria from electrical burns → increase fluids, target UO 75–100 mL/hr
  • GI: Curling's ulcer (stress ulcer) — H2 blocker/PPI prophylaxis mandatory; paralytic ileus first 24–48 hrs

Rehabilitation phase

Scar maturation: 6–24 months. Compression garments, silicone sheets, splinting/ROM for contracture prevention. Early psychological support — body image, PTSD.
